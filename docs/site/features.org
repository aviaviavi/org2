  <section class="org2-page-intro">
    <p>Org2 is an open-source follow-through system for people who work with AI agents. It keeps the source behind a decision, delegates work with bounded context, brings back the decisions that need you, and records the finished result in local plain-text files you own.</p>
    <div class="org2-page-summary" aria-label="Org2 feature summary">
      <div><strong>Decisions keep their source.</strong><span>Meetings, notes, tasks, links, and data stay attached to the work they create.</span></div>
      <div><strong>Work can move.</strong><span>People and named agents can act from the same context without hiding ownership or review boundaries.</span></div>
      <div><strong>Results come back.</strong><span>Finished artifacts and evidence return to a corpus that outlasts any model, harness, editor, or app.</span></div>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section class="org2-feature-section" id="compiler-loop" aria-labelledby="compiler-loop-title">
    <div class="org2-feature-heading">
      <h2 id="compiler-loop-title">The follow-through loop</h2>
      <p>Org2 carries a decision from its source through delegated work, human review, and a verifiable outcome.</p>
    </div>
    <div class="org2-compiler-flow" aria-label="Org2 compiler workflow">
      <article><strong>Capture</strong><p>Keep the commitment and its exact meeting, note, message, task, or dataset together.</p></article>
      <article><strong>Delegate</strong><p>Give a person or agent the bounded context and outcome they need.</p></article>
      <article><strong>Review</strong><p>Collect clarifications and consequential actions in one decision queue.</p></article>
      <article><strong>Verify</strong><p>Return outputs, validation, and evidence to the durable source record.</p></article>
    </div>
    <p class="org2-feature-note">Underneath, one parser turns ordinary files into shared semantics for the Mac app, VS Code, CLI tools, agent integrations, and publishing. Each client reads the same corpus instead of inventing its own interpretation.</p>
  </section>

  <section class="org2-feature-story" id="planning-knowledge" aria-labelledby="planning-knowledge-title">
    <div class="org2-feature-copy">
      <h2 id="planning-knowledge-title">Planning and linked notes</h2>
      <p>TODO state, schedules, deadlines, priorities, effort, tags, IDs, aliases, and links remain readable in the note. Org2 turns them into focused agenda views and a navigable knowledge graph.</p>
      <ul>
        <li>Agenda across many files, with date windows, filters, grouping, and real-world TODO vocabularies.</li>
        <li>Stable IDs, backlinks, dailies, node creation, link previews, and graph-health checks.</li>
        <li>Preview-first mutations from the CLI and editors; writes require an explicit apply step.</li>
      </ul>
      <div class="org2-command" aria-label="Planning and graph commands">
        <span>Planning and graph commands</span>
        <pre class="org2-src language-sh"><code class="language-sh">org2 agenda --dir ~/notes --recursive --tui
org2 roam graph --dir ~/notes --recursive --format report</code></pre>
      </div>

  <section class="org2-feature-story org2-feature-story-reverse" id="agent-work" aria-labelledby="agent-work-title">
    <div class="org2-feature-copy">
      <h2 id="agent-work-title">Decisions become durable work</h2>
      <p>Selected context, citations, plans, outputs, validation, clarifications, and approvals stay together while the work moves. People can see what happened before approving a consequential action—and what evidence proves it finished.</p>
      <ul>
        <li>Bounded context packs carry file-and-line citations instead of an opaque prompt dump.</li>
        <li>Portable goals and named agent profiles separate why and who from OpenClaw or Codex runtime identity; resolved refs remain attached to delegated headings, runs, and workflows.</li>
        <li>Generated work lands in reviewable artifact zones before promotion into canonical notes.</li>
        <li>One pending-decision queue combines run-bound approvals with standalone file-backed approvals; run decisions preserve one native ID and immutable review-material fingerprint across CLI, Mac, mobile, and OpenClaw. Decisions are item-scoped, so rejecting one action does not discard sibling approvals from the same batch.</li>
        <li>Recurring workflow executions roll up under stable logical work while retaining separate scheduler attempts, and event/fresh-path gates skip unchanged expensive work.</li>
        <li>A read-only workspace doctor finds contradictory run and approval state, duplicate decision identities, broken projections, and unmanaged recurring work before another agent acts.</li>
        <li>Atomic revision-checked run and workflow writes reject stale clients and ambiguous direct edits instead of silently replacing newer work.</li>
        <li>Per-account work ledgers keep stable identity, curated context, linked approvals, contact receipts, and idempotent history queryable without one ever-growing agent file.</li>
        <li>OpenClaw is the first native end-to-end integration; CLI, MCP, and plain files keep the model provider-independent.</li>
      </ul>
      <div class="org2-command" aria-label="Agent context and review commands">
        <span>Context and review commands</span>
        <pre class="org2-src language-sh"><code class="language-sh">org2 agent context --query "launch risks" --dir ~/notes --recursive
org2 doctor --dir ~/notes --json
org2 ledger list account-outreach --eligible --dir ~/notes --json
org2 approvals --dir ~/notes --recursive --format json
org2 review list --status pending --dir ~/notes</code></pre>
      </div>

  <section class="org2-feature-story" id="multi-agent-chat" aria-labelledby="multi-agent-chat-title">
    <div class="org2-feature-copy">
      <h2 id="multi-agent-chat-title">One conversation, many agents</h2>
      <p>A shared AI room lets you bring Codex, OpenClaw, and any named local or remote destination into one corpus-grounded conversation. Every participant sees the same bounded transcript, keeps its own identity and model, and answers inside one visible agent round.</p>
      <ul>
        <li>Mention <code>@codex-local</code>, <code>@codex-remote</code>, <code>@research-agent</code>, or any destination you configure; <code>@all</code> asks every participant.</li>
        <li>Responses stay grouped beneath the question that invoked them, with separate progress, model selection, and failure state for each agent.</li>
        <li>Mentioning another agent from a single-agent thread automatically forks it into a shared room, leaving the original conversation untouched.</li>
        <li>Agents can disagree, compare approaches, or propose explicit handoffs without Org2 hiding an autonomous loop behind the interface.</li>
      </ul>
      <p class="org2-feature-links"><a href="editors-macos.html#ai-chat-and-agents">Shared AI rooms</a><a href="getting-started.html#current-agent-setup-openclaw">Connect an agent</a></p>

  <section class="org2-feature-story" id="data-notebooks" aria-labelledby="data-notebooks-title">
    <div class="org2-feature-copy">
      <h2 id="data-notebooks-title">Data notebooks keep evidence close</h2>
      <p>Org2 notes can declare local or configured remote datasets, reusable SQL views, materialized results, freshness policy, and deterministic charts. Refresh is explicit; merely opening or publishing a document never runs a warehouse query.</p>
      <ul>
        <li>DuckDB-backed CSV, Parquet, JSON, table, ClickHouse, and Metabase inputs.</li>
        <li>Provenance-stamped result tables and source-aware chart definitions.</li>
        <li>Named external credential profiles; inline secrets are rejected.</li>
      </ul>
      <div class="org2-command" aria-label="Data notebook commands">
        <span>Inspect or materialize a result</span>
        <pre class="org2-src language-sh"><code class="language-sh">org2 query-data --file notes/readiness.org2 --inspect
org2 query-data --file notes/readiness.org2 --results weekly --apply</code></pre>

  <section class="org2-feature-story org2-feature-story-reverse" id="meetings-context" aria-labelledby="meetings-context-title">
    <div class="org2-feature-copy">
      <h2 id="meetings-context-title">Meetings become source-backed context</h2>
      <p>The macOS Workspace can record or import a meeting, keep audio and transcripts local, and render summaries, decisions, and follow-ups from ordinary files. Those records can then feed agenda items, cited agent runs, entity pages, and reports.</p>
      <ul>
        <li>Local-first transcription with explicit artifact paths and status.</li>
        <li>A Mac-owned completion hook can route every newly finished meeting to a named agent and new or existing thread, with idempotent restart reconciliation.</li>
        <li>Event and timeline metadata for connecting meetings, people, projects, and later work.</li>
        <li>Raw evidence, curated notes, generated drafts, and published output retain provenance.</li>
      </ul>

  <section class="org2-feature-section" id="external-sources" aria-labelledby="external-sources-title">
    <div class="org2-feature-heading">
      <h2 id="external-sources-title">Bring in context without surrendering control</h2>
      <p>Source profiles stage external material as inspectable captures with stable identity and provenance. The boundary is designed for ingestion plugins, so messages, documents, issues, mail, datasets, and future sources can enter the same reviewable flow.</p>
    </div>
    <div class="org2-feature-grid">
      <article><h3>Pluggable by design</h3><p>A source adapter supplies bounded records and provenance; Org2 supplies the shared staging, review, linking, and promotion semantics.</p></article>
      <article><h3>Local and inspectable</h3><p>Machine bindings and credentials stay outside canonical notes. Staged files retain stable source IDs, timestamps, hashes, URLs, and provenance.</p></article>
      <article><h3>Review before promotion</h3><p>Imported material lands under configured raw and views zones. People and agents can redact, verify, and promote selected knowledge without treating an external mirror as canonical truth.</p></article>
    </div>
  </section>
